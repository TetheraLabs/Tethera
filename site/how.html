How Tethera works

Watch. Count. Charge.

A live view of every automated wallet on the chain, a one-call signal that says how crowded a trade is right now, and a pool that charges the herd for its own impact.

Watch

Every Uniswap v2, v3 and v4 swap on Robinhood Chain is decoded within a second of the block. Each wallet is labelled by how it behaves, not by what it claims to be: same-block trades across pools, sub-second gaps, metronomic timing, computed sizes, reaction speed, persistence. Three or more of those, over seven days of history, and the wallet is automated. Trades that cancel themselves inside one transaction, both ways in one pool with nothing settled, are flagged and never counted.

Count

For any pool and direction, the signal returns how many automated wallets are on your side of the trade in the last sixty seconds, how many are on the other side, how one-sided the minute is, how many other pools are moving the same way, and where this minute ranks against the pool's own history. One word sums it up: clean, busy, or herd.

Charge

A Uniswap v4 hook reads the public set of automated wallets and counts them per pool, per block. When a herd forms, the pool's fee rises for the herd's side and falls for whoever leans against it. Liquidity providers stop paying for the busiest minutes, the herd pays for its own impact, and every surcharged swap is written to the chain as a receipt.

The signal

One call, one answer.

A real response from the mainnet signal for the ETH/USDG v4 pool during the 17:04 UTC herd on 14 October 2026.

  • auto_wallets_same counts automated wallets selling in the last 60 seconds.
  • auto_wallets_opposite counts those buying. Close numbers mean arbitrage, not a herd.
  • one_sidedness runs from 0 (balanced) to 1 (everyone on one side).
  • pools_moving_same_way shows whether this is one pool or a wave.
  • percentile ranks this minute against every minute the pool has seen.

What we do not do

We do not front-run. The signal describes the last sixty seconds, not the next. The hook sets a pool's fee from swaps that have already landed; it never sees an order before the chain does. The automated-wallet set it reads is a public contract, so anyone can audit who is in it, and Tethera's share is a fifth of the surcharge only, capped in the code.
