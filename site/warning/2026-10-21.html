How the trick works

Uniswap v4 settles a transaction's swaps net, at the end. Swap $5 million of USDG into a pool and $5 million back out in the same transaction and you owe the pool nothing, the pool owes you nothing, and two Swap events of $5 million each are written to the chain all the same. In a pool with a 0.3% fee that costs $30,000 a round trip, so nobody does it. In a pool with a zero fee it costs the gas.

So you create a token, pair it with real USDG in a zero-fee pool, provide the liquidity yourself, and swap against your own pool in a loop. One transaction this week, 0xa6e8cc05ea7a0538aa4907014b93853727f6ae0de764dedd3ea6b6a75adde5d3, contains 80 alternating swaps of exactly $5 million: $400 million of reported volume, nothing moved. Three pools were taken to exactly $500 million, $500 million and $600 million.

We flag every transaction that swaps both ways in one pool with both token amounts netting to under 1% of the gross, and we call the result self-cancelling volume: trades that transfer nothing between any two parties. What the author meant by it is their business.

Two senders, then: 0xa307B4C7… and 0x3F5D6D4a…. In the ten largest cases we checked the pools' liquidity events as well: the wallet doing the round trips is the wallet that supplied the pool's liquidity in every one, and in seven of the ten it is the only liquidity provider. Other wallets did trade in those pools, mostly bots sniffing new pairs, to the tune of $315 thousand against $992 million of round trips over the week we first measured.

Outside zero-fee pools the pattern is essentially absent: $13 million in $3 billion. In the seven weeks from 29 June to 16 August, $6.5 billion of USDG-quoted volume, the share was 0.0% in every week and every fee tier. This is an October operation, and it may stop as soon as it is noticed. The flag stays on regardless.

Why it matters to you

If your agent ranks pools by volume, it has been ranking a self-dealing pool above every real market on the chain. If you were reading HOOKR's, STANDARD's or BONER's dollar volume this week, 98%, 99% and 93% of what you saw in their USDG pools was one wallet talking to itself. If you are deciding where to provide liquidity, the busiest pool on your screen may have no counterparties at all.

Fake dollars, too

The first thing we had to remove was not the round trips. It was the dollars. Twenty-four tokens on this chain call themselves USDC, USDG or USDT. One is the Global Dollar. Ten of the twelve "USDC"s are a token named UpSide Down Cat with eighteen decimals; priced naively, one of its pools showed $400 billion in a week. Ninety tokens call themselves SPCX.

We now keep one token per symbol, the one in the most pools, and mark the rest as copies. For stocks we go further: Robinhood publishes a registry of its Stock Token contracts, and only those count as the real thing. There are 195 of them on the chain.

What we do about it

Every swap in our data carries a self-cancelling flag. Every number Tethera publishes, every herd, every wallet label, every pool figure, is computed without them. The pool endpoint of the API returns both numbers, organic and printed, so you can check our work pool by pool: api.tetheralabs.com/v1/pool/{pool}.
